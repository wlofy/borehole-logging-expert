## 1. Drilling methods and codes

| Method | Typical codes | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid-stem auger | SSA, AV (V-bit), AT (tungsten-carbide bit) | Dry, competent soils; samples disturbed; depths of grabs approximate |
| Hollow-stem auger | HSA, HOA | Augers act as casing; sampling through the stem; difficult in gravel |
| Wash boring / mud rotary | WB, washbore, RM (mud), RC/RW (water), RA (air) | Fluid keeps hole open below water; masks water levels |
| Rock coring | HQ, NQ, PQ, NMLC, triple tube | See `03` |
| Tricone / rock roller, drag bit | RR, DB | Advances without core |
| Air track / percussion probe | | Rate of advance gives rock depth; chips only |
| Sonic | | Continuous core; vibration disturbance |
| Hand auger | HA | Shallow; refusal depth informative |
| Test pit / trench | TP, BH (backhoe), EX (excavator) | Logged from the walls; no SPT |
| Hydrovac / daylighting | | Clears services in the top metres; no description possible there |
| Casing support | C (casing), M (mud), W (water); casing sizes HW, NW | Record casing depth as advanced |

Methods change with depth in one hole ("SSA to 6 m, then mud rotary, then HQ
coring"). Store method per depth range, not one value per hole.

## 2. Drilling observations worth recording

- Rate of advance: easy/hard drilling, sudden change (often a boundary).
- Rig behaviour: chatter or grinding (cobbles, boulders), rods dropping (voids,
  very soft zones), auger refusal, casing refusal, spoon bouncing.
- Drilling fluid: type, loss of circulation and depth, % water return.
- Heave or running sand, caving, squeezing.
- Odour, gas, artesian flow.
- Times: start and finish, run times, overnight standing (water levels change).
- Bit type changes (useful strength indicators in weak rock).
- Measurement-while-drilling data where the rig records torque, thrust and
  penetration rate.

Why it matters: in poor recovery these notes are often the only evidence of where
a boundary is or why a sample is missing.

## 3. Hole geometry and investigation types

- Vertical boreholes from surface (the default assumption).
- Inclined holes: bearing and angle from horizontal; depths along the hole
  (`03` §9).
- Downhole survey tables for deep holes that drift.
- Test pits and trenches: dimensions, wall stability, seepage, photos per wall.
- Hand auger and probe holes.
- CPT soundings (data files, no samples).
- Offset or paired holes after refusal ("BH-2B drilled 2 m east of BH-2").
- Over-water holes (water depth, mudline) and underground holes (collar in mine
  coordinates, upward dips).
- Coordinates: UTM zone or site grid; elevation with datum (e.g. a geodetic
  datum vs a local benchmark) and how it was obtained (survey, GPS/RTK,
  estimated).

## 4. Groundwater

Record every observation with depth, date, time and stage:

| Stage | Meaning | Reliability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water strike / first encountered | Depth at which water entered while drilling | Often rises afterwards; record rise and time |
| During drilling / through casing | Level inside casing or augers | Affected by drilling water |
| On completion / end of day | Level when drilling stops, with cave-in depth | Not stabilised |
| Next day / 24 h | After standing | Better |
| Stabilised / monitoring | From a well or piezometer over time | Best |
| Seepage | Slow inflow, no measurable level | |
| Dry / not encountered / not observed | Different meanings: dry hole vs not checked | Do not conflate |
| Cave-in / sloughing depth | Where the hole collapsed | Water may be at or above it |
| Artesian / flowing | Head above ground (record height) | Design-critical |
| Perched water | Water above a low-permeability layer | Multiple levels possible |

Pitfalls:
- Wash boring and mud rotary hide groundwater; a level measured with drilling
  fluid in the hole is not a groundwater level.
- In clays, water may take days to stabilise; a "dry on completion" clay hole
  says little.
- Weather and season matter; note recent rain.
- Soil moisture (`02` §7) and water level are separate observations.

## 5. Wells, standpipes and piezometers

Monitoring well or standpipe, recorded as depth intervals from top down:

| Component | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Protective casing / cover | Stick-up (height above ground) or flush mount; lock |
| Riser pipe | Material (PVC), diameter, depth to top of screen |
| Screen | Depth from–to, slot size, diameter |
| Filter / sand pack | Depth from–to, sand grade |
| Bentonite seal | Depth from–to (chips, pellets) |
| Grout / backfill | Depth from–to, material |
| Elevations | Ground, top of pipe (TOP/TOC) |
| Development | Volume purged, method, clarity |

Vibrating-wire piezometers: tip depth, serial number, grouted-in or in sand,
readings over time. Inclinometer casing: depth, A-axis orientation. Thermistor
strings: bead depths (northern projects).

Final logs usually draw the installation as a column beside the log. A screen
depth is needed to interpret any water level from that well.

## 6. Backfill, decommissioning and termination

- Termination: depth and reason (target depth reached, auger refusal, spoon
  refusal, refusal on probable bedrock or suspected boulder, hole collapse,
  obstruction, safety). The reason changes how a designer reads the bottom of
  the log.
- Backfill: material per depth range (cuttings, bentonite chips, grout, sand);
  some jurisdictions require a decommissioning record.
- Surface reinstatement (asphalt patch, concrete).
- Completion notes are usually numbered under the final log.

## 7. Environmental field screening

- PID or FID headspace readings (ppm) per sample; record instrument and lamp.
- Odour, staining, sheen, free product, debris in fill.
- Sample containers and preservation for lab analysis; chain of custody.
- Field water quality during well development (pH, EC, temperature, turbidity).

These are observations; contamination conclusions belong in the environmental
report.

## 8. Frozen ground

In northern and permafrost projects, logs add frozen-soil descriptions (often
following an ASTM D4083 / NRC-style scheme): visible ice absent or present, ice
type and content (e.g. Nf, Nbn, Vx, Vs, ICE), thaw sensitivity, ground
temperature from thermistors, depth of active layer. Treat as a specialist
extension; confirm the scheme with the firm.
